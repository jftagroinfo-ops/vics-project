Indian Sesame Seeds: Natural vs Hulled Grades for Importers

Updated 12 August 2026 7 min read JFT Agro Trade Desk

Sesame offers can look similar while differing materially in purity, colour, microbiology and processing suitability. A precise specification protects the buyer far better than ordering only by the words “natural” or “hulled.”

Choose the grade around its end use

Natural sesame retains its outer hull and is commonly used for crushing, seasoning and applications where a stronger sesame character is preferred. Hulled sesame is mechanically processed to remove the outer layer and is often selected for bakery, tahini and visually light products. The buyer should also state whether mechanically hulled or another processing method is acceptable.

Microbiology and residue planning

Sesame intended for ready-to-eat or low-kill-step applications requires especially clear microbiological expectations. Define the organism, limit, sample size, laboratory method and responsibility for retesting. Pesticide residue requirements should be matched to the destination market before procurement because acceptable limits are not universal.

Do not assume a certificate from a previous lot proves the current cargo. Reports should identify the actual lot, test date, method and laboratory. Where risk or contract value warrants it, arrange independent sampling before loading.

Details to put in the purchase order

  • Natural or hulled grade and intended end use.
  • Minimum purity and maximum moisture.
  • Colour, damaged seed and foreign-matter tolerances.
  • Microbiological, pesticide and contaminant requirements.
  • Bag type, liner, net weight, marking and pallet policy.
  • Inspection, sampling, sealing and document responsibilities.

Packing and shelf-life controls

Sesame is commonly shipped in lined food-grade bags. Packaging should limit moisture entry, withstand stacking and carry traceable lot markings. Before loading, check container odour, previous-cargo risk, water ingress, floor condition and door seals. Desiccants may support moisture management but do not replace dry cargo and a sound container.

Comparing supplier offers

First align the grade, purity, test scope, packing, Incoterm and destination. Then compare the total landed proposition. A quotation that includes destination-specific testing and robust packing may carry more value than a lower offer with vague quality language.

Buyer note

Import restrictions and food-safety controls change by origin, product and destination. Reconfirm the current rules with the competent authority and your customs or food-safety adviser before shipment.
