Indian Coriander Seeds: Eagle vs Scooter Grade Explained

Updated 12 August 2026 7 min read JFT Agro Trade Desk

Eagle and Scooter are commercial trade descriptions, not complete specifications. Importers should translate the requested grade into measurable requirements for colour, splits, purity, moisture, volatile oil, residues and packing.

What Eagle and Scooter usually communicate

In Indian trade, Eagle is generally offered as a bolder, more uniform and visually premium seed, while Scooter is commonly positioned as a value grade with a different size and appearance profile. These names alone do not guarantee a standard result across suppliers. Approve a physical sample and record measurable tolerances.

Use a sealed approval sample

Ask for a representative sample from the offered lot or crop position, not a hand-selected display sample. After approval, seal and sign two matching samples: one retained by the buyer and one by the supplier. The contract should say whether final inspection is based on visual comparison, laboratory results or both.

For ground coriander, add mesh size, microbiology, adulteration controls and treatment status. Whole-seed and powder specifications should not be treated as interchangeable.

Testing and destination compliance

Spices Board India lists quality standards and testing resources for exported spices. Buyers should also provide destination-specific residue and contaminant limits because an origin-market report may not cover every requirement in the importing country. Review the Spices Board quality information and confirm the final test panel with an accredited laboratory.

Before confirming the order

  • State whole seed or powder and intended application.
  • Approve grade, colour, crop and split tolerance.
  • Define purity, moisture and volatile-oil requirements.
  • List pesticide, contaminant and microbiology tests.
  • Agree bag, liner, net weight, lot marking and inspection scope.

Packing and arrival quality

Lined food-grade bags are common for bulk coriander. The container must be dry, odour-free and suitable for food cargo. Loading photographs, lot markings and seal records create a simple evidence trail. On arrival, compare the lot with the retained sample before mixing it into production stock.

Practical rule

Do not buy a trade name. Buy a written specification, an approved sample and a defined inspection process.
