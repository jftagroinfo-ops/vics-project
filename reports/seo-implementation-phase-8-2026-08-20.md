# SEO Implementation Phase 8 — 20 August 2026

## Outcome

This phase addressed opportunity-map ranks 32–36: milling wheat, yellow maize, maize grits, corn flour and corn starch.

## Implemented work

### Milling wheat

- Removed generic premium, port, certification and international-standards language from metadata and hero copy.
- Explained that “milling wheat” describes an intended use, not a complete grade.
- Added RFQ fields for common versus durum wheat, intended flour output, test weight, protein basis, moisture, falling-number method, defects, sampling and mill trials.
- Replaced unsupported fixed-purity and universal-document language with contracted cleaning, sampling and destination-specific evidence.
- Connected raw wheat to the whole-wheat flour, maida and purchase-specification paths.

### Yellow maize

- Separated food, animal-feed and dry/wet-processing enquiries.
- Required distinct contaminant, residue, microbiological, treatment, GMO/declaration, establishment and document checks according to intended use.
- Clarified that chemically treated seed is a separate route and must not be represented as food or feed.
- Linked the raw maize page to grits, maize flour and corn starch so buyers can choose the intended processed output.

### Maize grits

- Replaced generic granulation and use-case language with a full retained-and-passing sieve distribution workflow.
- Added degermed identity, bran/germ removal, composition, safety scope and application-trial fields.
- Made snack, extrusion, cereal and brewing references enquiry contexts rather than suitability promises.
- Used current FSSAI and Codex grits terminology.

### Corn flour and corn starch

- Positioned the corn-flour page around ground maize flour rather than isolated starch.
- Added a direct comparison of ground flour, starch and grits to reduce cross-market terminology ambiguity.
- Positioned the starch page around native maize starch; modified starch is explicitly outside that identity.
- Added a food-industry pathway covering analytical and microbiological release tests, application trials, moisture-barrier packing and shipment evidence.
- Added reciprocal flour-versus-starch links and linked both pages to raw maize or grits where relevant.

## Scope boundary

The copy does not guarantee milling performance, food/feed suitability, granulation, contaminant acceptance, application performance, certificate availability or destination approval. These depend on identity, written specifications, current regulations, named test methods, representative samples and buyer trials.
