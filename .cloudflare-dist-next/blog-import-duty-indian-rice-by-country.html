Indian Rice Import Duty: A Country-by-Country Verification Guide

A rice duty rate is not reliable without the destination tariff code, origin, processing form, customs value, shipment date and any preference, quota or trade measure. This guide links to current official lookup tools instead of copying rates that may expire.

Why one “rice duty” number is misleading

Chapter 10 tariff schedules distinguish paddy, husked rice, semi-milled or wholly milled rice, parboiled forms and broken rice. National tariff lines may add further distinctions. Preferential treatment can also depend on originating status and documentary proof, while quotas, safeguards, VAT and other levies may sit outside the headline customs duty.

Do not assume preference: a trade agreement does not make every rice line duty-free. Confirm that the product meets the applicable origin rule and that the required evidence will be accepted for the shipment.

A reproducible verification workflow

  1. Classify the exact rice product with the importer or licensed customs professional.
  2. Enter the complete destination tariff code and Indian origin in the official tool.
  3. Check duty, preference, quota, safeguard, licence, document and effective-date notes.
  4. Confirm the customs valuation basis and destination exchange-rate rule.
  5. Add VAT or GST, levies, inspection, broker, port, storage and inland-delivery costs separately.
  6. Save a dated result or written broker confirmation with the landed-cost model.
  7. Recheck shortly before dispatch because measures can change after quotation.

Landed-cost model

Customs value + customs duty + import VAT/GST + other statutory levies + inspection and clearance charges + port/storage costs + inland delivery = estimated landed cost.

Use variables from the current official lookup and the importer’s broker rather than a static worked example. JFT’s CIF quotation is a commercial input; it is not a customs ruling or a guarantee of destination charges.
